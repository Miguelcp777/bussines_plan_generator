How this works

  1. 1Load the SAP IB Data export. Columns are detected automatically.
  2. 2Add the Service Reclass file so bundled revenue is counted too. Matched by serial number.
  3. 3Review data quality and set the BP year, currency and recognition convention.
  4. 4Decide renewal per customer-system record and set probabilities.
  5. 5Log risks and opportunities with evidence, owner and event date.
  6. 6Read the scenarios, then generate and freeze the executive report.

Revenue recognition

Continuation matches Appendix A of the methodology: the baseline holds the full year and non-renewal is captured as a weighted risk. Contractual is the conservative finance view.
Contracts ended longer ago than this are excluded from the baseline and reported separately as recovery opportunities.
100% by default: the plan assumes renewal until you decide otherwise, rather than inventing a churn rate. Lower it only if you want a blanket assumption across every untouched contract.

Bundle revenue · Service Reclass

contracts the customer does not pay directly
Turning it off does not unload the file; every figure simply drops back to what the customer pays directly.
A reclass figure is a quarter annualised, not a dated contract. Run rate is the honest default; the coverage option is the conservative one, and it also exposes bundle revenue to the renewal probability.
ℹ
Matching is by serial number. A row counts as bundled when Final Treatment after adj reads Reclass; the value taken is Annualized Q (for budget), already annualised and in USD. Rows marked - are paid by the customer and their revenue is already in the installed base — they are not added again.

Coverage

what counts as a covered system, and the target to hit
T&M is ticked by default: it is not a contract, it is what a system falls back to without one. Everything left unticked counts as covered.
Leave at 0 for no target. With a target set, the KPI shows how many systems short you are.
